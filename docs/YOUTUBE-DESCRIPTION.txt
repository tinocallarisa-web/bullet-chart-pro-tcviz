A number next to its target does not tell you whether it is good. 92,500 against a target of
78,000 — is that a great month or just an ordinary one? The native way in Power BI is a
clustered bar, a variance measure in DAX and a rule for the colours, and it still does not say
how far you are from "good".

This walkthrough builds it with Bullet Chart Pro: actual against target on one line, the
variance and its percentage calculated for you, qualitative bands behind every bar — no DAX.

WHAT YOU'LL SEE
• Category + Actual + Target: the variance and % appear on every row, green or red
• Qualitative bands: what "poor", "fair" and "good" look like behind each bar
• Bands from your own measures (Pro): every row with its own thresholds, calculated in DAX
• A second target (Pro): the budget and the stretch goal side by side, each with its variance
• Small multiples (Pro): one panel per country or year, on a shared scale
• The setup guide inside the visual: try the Pro features on your own data
• Style templates — Clean, Minimal, Contrast, Soft, Compact and an IBCS style — and the legend
• Format on the canvas: click the bar, the target or the legend to format it
• Small tiles, vertical bars and the visual in 12 languages

FREE AND PRO
The free version is a complete bullet chart: variance and %, qualitative bands, conditional
formatting on the bar, reference lines, labels, legend, drilldown and cross-filtering, style
templates, the setup guide, format on the canvas and 12 languages. Pro adds bands from your own
measures, a second target, small multiples, the forecast marker and vertical bars — and the
setup guide lets you try them on your own data before buying, under a "Pro preview" watermark
while you edit.

PRIVACY
No network requests, no telemetry: everything is calculated inside Power BI. The links on the
start screen open only when you click them.
