Bullet Chart Pro shows each actual value against its target on one line: the bar is the actual, the marker is the target, the bands say how far the value is from good, and the variance (absolute and %) is calculated by the visual — no DAX measure needed for it.

Quick start

  1. Add the visual from Microsoft AppSource (Power BI Desktop → Get more visuals).
  2. Drag a dimension (region, product, team, month) into Category: one bar per value.
  3. Drag the measure you track into Actual (Real). The bars appear.
  4. Drag the goal — budget, plan or last year — into Target.
  5. Variance (absolute + %) is calculated automatically and coloured green or red.

While you are editing, the setup guide opens over the chart and walks you through these steps, the Pro features and the styles. Close it with ✕ or Finish.

Setup guide

The guide appears over the chart only while you edit a report, once the visual has data, and not in tiles smaller than 360 × 220 px (there it would cover the whole chart).

  1. Your data — Category, Actual and Target; Target gets a tick once it is in its well. The list updates by itself as you drag fields.
  2. Try Pro on your data — a Try button switches to vertical bars with one click. For bands from measures, the second target and small multiples, the guide tells you which well to use and ticks the item once it is filled. Without a licence they show under the watermark while you edit.
  3. Pick a style — the seven collection styles, each with a thumbnail.

Close it with ✕ or Finish. To open it again: Format → Style template → Setup guide = On.

Styles

Format → Style template → Template, or step 3 of the guide. Bullet Chart Pro uses the same seven styles as the other TCViz visuals (Box & Whisker, Radar, Treemap, Line Chart), so a page built with several of them reads as one report. All styles are free.

  • A style only fills what you have not set yourself: a colour you picked by hand wins.
  • Picking a style replaces the colours any style defines: bar, target, second target, forecast, positive and negative variance, the three bands, legend text, small-multiple panel colours and the focus colour. Ledger and Editorial also set a serif font. Everything else stays as you left it.
  • The dark styles (Boardroom and Aurora) paint the background of the visual.
  • High contrast always wins over the style.

On-object formatting

With on-object formatting turned on in Power BI Desktop, you can click these elements on the canvas and format them from the menu that opens: the actual bar, the target, the second target, the forecast, the bands, the labels, the variance, the legend, the axis, the reference lines and the panel titles in small multiples. Each menu has shortcuts and a link to its card in the format pane. The axis offers Show scale axis, Gridlines and Axis title toggles; the small-multiple titles offer Panel border.

In Desktop the first click selects the visual and the second selects the element. While formatting, clicks do not filter and tooltips are not shown.

Accessibility

Keyboard. Select the visual, then ← → ↑ ↓ move between bars with a visible focus ring, Enter selects the bar (and filters the page), Esc clears the selection. The colour and width of the focus ring are in Format → Accessibility.

Screen readers. Each row is read in full: category, actual, target, variance (amount and %) and, if present, the second target — with the formats and names of your measures.

High contrast. In a Windows high contrast theme the bars, markers, bands, axis, legend, guide and start screen take their colours from the theme (Format → Accessibility → High contrast = Automatic, the default). Set it to On to draw the chart black on white even without the Windows theme — for printing, projectors or readers who need it. High contrast always wins over the style.

Minimum text size. Format → Accessibility → Minimum text size [6]: no text of the visual — labels, legend, axis, reference-line labels, panel titles — is drawn smaller, even in small tiles. Raise it for projected or printed reports.

Languages and number formats

The format pane, the field wells, the start screen, the guide, the legend, the tooltip and the notices are available in 12 languages: English, Spanish, Portuguese (Brazil), Portuguese (Portugal), French, German, Italian, Dutch, Polish, Japanese, Chinese (Simplified) and Korean. The visual follows the language of Power BI; any other language falls back to English.

Numbers use the format string of each measure in your model — currency, thousands, decimals, percentages — including dynamic format strings (each row uses the dynamic format of its measure) and calculation groups, and the decimal and thousands separators of the report language. A measure with no format is shortened to K / M.

Use cases

1. Plan vs actual by region

Category = Region · Actual = [Sales] · Target = [Budget]. Each region reads in one line: how much it sold, where the budget is and the gap in money and %. Turn on Show target value if the reader needs the budget figure, set Green/red threshold to a small negative number to tolerate near misses, and Sort → Variance % to put the worst misses at one end.

With Pro, put Year in Small Multiples By to see one panel per year with a shared scale, or switch to vertical bars when the category is a month.

2. Operational KPIs with thresholds per row Pro

Different KPIs — tickets closed, on-time delivery, first-call resolution — each have their own idea of “poor” and “good”. Percentages of the data maximum cannot express that; band measures can:

Band 1 limit = SUM ( KPI[Target] ) * 0.8
Band 2 limit = SUM ( KPI[Target] ) * 0.95
Band maximum = SUM ( KPI[Target] ) * 1.2

Each row now has bands at 80%, 95% and 120% of its own target. You can also read the limits from a table of thresholds maintained by the business, e.g. MAX ( Thresholds[Poor] ).

3. Target plus stretch goal Pro

Target = [Budget] · Second target = [Stretch] (or [Sales LY]). The second target is a hollow marker, so the two never get confused. The tooltip shows both values and the variance against each one, named after your measures.

Stretch = [Budget] * 1.1
Sales LY = CALCULATE ( [Sales], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR ( 'Date'[Date] ) )

Frequently asked questions

Getting started

What is the difference between Free and Pro?

Free is the complete bullet chart: actual vs target, automatic variance, qualitative bands, the seven styles, sorting, reference lines, colours per category and by rule (bar and target), axis title and gridlines, row height, tooltips, drill-down, cross-filtering, bookmarks, keyboard navigation, the Accessibility card, on-object formatting and 12 languages. Pro adds five things: small multiples, the forecast marker, vertical orientation, a second target and bands from measures.

Do I need to write a DAX measure for the variance?

No. With Actual and Target in their wells, the visual calculates Actual − Target and the % of the target. DAX is only needed if you want bands from measures or a second target that you calculate yourself.

The visual only shows the start screen

It needs a measure in Actual (Real). Add a category and a target too. The links on the start screen (guide, product page, video) open only when you click them.

How do I reopen the setup guide?

Format → Style template → Setup guide = On. It only appears while you edit, with data in the visual, and in tiles of at least 360 × 220 px. It never appears in reading view.

Styles

I picked a style and my colours changed

That is by design: picking a style replaces the colours any style defines (bar, target, second target, forecast, variance, bands, legend text, panel colours, focus colour), so that you can see it. Settings no style defines — sizes, widths, sort, toggles — stay as they were. After picking a style you can still change any colour by hand; your colour wins over the style.

How do I go back to the look I had before picking a style?

Pick Report theme: it clears the colours the previous style set, and the chart follows the report theme again. Colours you had set by hand before picking the style are not restored; set them again, or undo (Ctrl+Z) in Power BI Desktop.

Why is the background dark?

Boardroom and Aurora are dark styles and paint the background of the visual. Pick a light style (Report theme, Ledger, Editorial, Signal or Terrace) for a light background.

Where are Clean, Minimal, Contrast, Soft and Compact?

The six 1.5 templates were replaced in 1.6 by the seven collection styles shared with the other TCViz visuals. A report saved with one of them opens with the closest style: Clean, Minimal, Soft and Compact → Report theme (Minimal keeps the scale axis off, Compact its 9 px labels and 8 px legend), Contrast → Boardroom, IBCS style → Signal. Nothing to do on your side.

Where did IBCS mode go?

The switch was removed from Format → General in 1.6; General now only has Orientation. Reports saved with the former IBCS mode on (and no template chosen) open in the Signal style, the sober look closest to it.

Do styles cost extra?

No. All seven styles are free.

Data & variance

Why is the variance not showing in a small tile?

When the values column is too narrow, the visual decides for the whole column what fits, in this order: the target value is dropped first, then the variance amount (the % stays), then the variance. Nothing is lost: hover over the bar and the tooltip shows the actual, the target and the variance in full. Widen the visual, reduce Category column width %, or set Values panel width %.

Why is the variance not showing at all?

Both Actual and Target must have a value for that row, and Format → Variance → Show variance label must be on.

How does the green/red threshold work?

It is in the units of your measure. A variance equal to or above the threshold is positive (green ▲); below it, negative (red ▼). 0 means “at or above target”. −5 tolerates misses of up to 5 units.

How do I set bands from measures? Pro

Create one, two or three measures and drag them into Band 1 limit, Band 2 limit and Band maximum. For example:

Band 1 limit = SUM ( T[Target] ) * 0.9
Band 2 limit = SUM ( T[Target] )
Band maximum = SUM ( T[Target] ) * 1.2

Each row draws its own bands. If you leave Band maximum empty, band 3 runs to the data maximum. Rows where the measures are blank fall back to the percentages in Format → Qualitative Bands. The limits are listed in the tooltip. Colours stay in Format → Colors.

What are the bands measured against?

Without band measures, the two thresholds are percentages of the largest value in the data (actual, target, forecast, second target), not of each row's target. Use them for “how good is this”, and the target marker for “did we hit it”. For per-row thresholds use band measures (Pro).

How do I add a second target? Pro

Drag a measure into Second target. It is drawn as a hollow marker in its own colour (Format → Colors → Second target marker), appears in the legend with your measure's name, and the tooltip adds its value and the variance against it.

Does it support negative values?

Yes. With negative values the scale includes zero and a zero baseline is drawn; bars to the left of it are negative.

Can I format numbers as currency, thousands, percentages?

Yes. The visual uses each measure's format string from the model, including dynamic format strings and calculation groups, so a measure can switch between €, $ and % per row or per selection. The separators follow the report language. A measure without a format is shortened to K / M.

How many categories can it show?

Up to 2,000. With many rows the chart scrolls instead of squeezing them; filter or use Top N for longer lists.

Sorting

How do I sort the bars by variance?

Format → Sort → Sort bars by = Variance (amount) or Variance % (relative to the target), and turn Descending on or off to choose which end the biggest misses go to. You can also sort by Actual, Target or Category name. Free.

The Power BI “Sort axis” menu does nothing

It works only with Sort bars by = Data order (the default). Any other choice in the Sort card decides the order inside the visual.

Where do rows without a value go when sorting?

Last, whatever the direction. In small multiples each panel is sorted the same way.

Layout & formatting

The rows look too thick or too thin. How do I change them?

Two settings in Format → Layout and axis. Row height: with Fit to visual (default) the rows share the whole height of the visual, so a few rows in a tall visual are tall; choose Fixed and set Fixed row height (px) (16–200, default 40) to keep the same height whatever the visual size — if the rows do not fit, the visual scrolls. Bar thickness % of row (10–90, default 44) sets how much of the row the bar fills; raise it for thicker bars. In vertical orientation it sets the column width.

Since 1.6 my rows are taller and fill the visual

Before 1.6 the rows were capped at 72 px and left empty space below. Fit to visual now uses the whole height. For the old, tighter look, set Row height = Fixed with the height you want.

How do I add an axis title or hide the gridlines?

Format → Layout and axis → Axis title = On; leave Axis title text empty to use the measure name, or type your own. Gridlines turns the axis gridlines on or off, and Show scale axis hides the whole axis. On the canvas, the axis on-object menu has the same three toggles.

How does on-object formatting work? Why do I need two clicks?

In Power BI Desktop with on-object formatting on, the first click selects the visual and the second click selects the element under the pointer — bar, target, second target, forecast, bands, labels, variance, legend, axis, reference lines or panel titles. Its menu has the main options and a link to its card in the format pane. While formatting, clicks do not filter and tooltips are hidden.

How do I use small multiples? Pro

Drag a field into Small Multiples By: the chart splits into one panel per value. In Format → Small Multiples set the columns (0 = automatic; fewer are used if the visual is too narrow), the space between panels, whether the scale is shared, the title size, colour, bold and alignment, and the panel background and border. The rows shrink down to 16 px so the panels fit inside the visual, and the legend stays visible.

How do I get vertical bars? Pro

Format → General → Orientation = Vertical, or Try in step 2 of the guide. Vertical reads well for months or long category names. The two width settings in Layout and axis are not used in vertical orientation; Bar thickness % of row sets the column width.

The values on the right are cut or missing

The bar always keeps at least 40% of the width, so the values column gives way first. Widen the visual, reduce Category column width % or set Values panel width %. Long category names end in “…”: widen Category column width % to show more of them.

Can I colour the bars by a rule instead of one by one?

Yes. Format → Colors → Actual bar has the fx button: drive the colour from a measure, e.g. red below target and green above. The rule is evaluated per category. A colour set by hand on one category overrides the rule for that category. Free.

Can I colour the target marker by a rule?

Yes, new in 1.6. Format → Colors → Target marker → fx: for example, a different colour for stretch targets or for targets set by a given team. The rule is evaluated per category. A fixed colour chosen next to fx applies to the whole visual. Free.

How do I add reference lines?

Format → Reference Lines → turn on Line 1 or Line 2 and set the value, label and colour. The labels sit in a strip above the rows; near the right edge a label moves to the left of its line. In small multiples the lines appear inside every panel.

Interaction

How does selection work?

Click a bar to filter the other visuals on the page; the other bars are dimmed. Ctrl+click adds or removes bars. Click the same bar again, or an empty area, to clear. Right-click opens Power BI's context menu.

Do bookmarks keep the selection?

Yes. A bookmark stores the selection and the filters; when you apply it, the selected bars are highlighted and the rest dimmed, as if you had clicked them.

Does it respect interactions being turned off?

Yes. The visual reads whether Power BI allows interactions with it; when they are not allowed, clicks and keys do not select or filter.

How do I drill down?

Put a hierarchy or several fields in Category and use Power BI's drill buttons on the visual header.

Accessibility

Can I use the keyboard?

Yes. Select the visual, then ← → ↑ ↓ move between bars with a visible focus ring, Enter selects, Escape clears. Change the ring's colour and width in Format → Accessibility.

What does a screen reader read?

Each row in full: category, actual, target, variance (amount and %) and second target, with your formats and measure names.

Does it support high contrast mode?

Yes. In a Windows high contrast theme the bars, markers, bands, axis, legend, guide and start screen take their colours from the theme.

Can I force high contrast without the Windows theme?

Yes. Format → Accessibility → High contrast = On draws the chart black on white whatever the Windows setting. Automatic (default) follows the Windows contrast theme.

Some text is too small to read

Format → Accessibility → Minimum text size (default 6): no text of the visual — labels, legend, axis, reference-line labels, panel titles — is drawn smaller than this, even when the tile is small. Raise it to 9 or 10 for projected or printed reports.

Languages, licensing & privacy

Which languages does it speak?

English, Spanish, Portuguese (Brazil and Portugal), French, German, Italian, Dutch, Polish, Japanese, Chinese (Simplified) and Korean. It follows the Power BI language; other languages fall back to English. Field names, titles and your measure names are shown as you wrote them.

How do I unlock Pro?

Buy the Pro plan on Microsoft AppSource and assign the licences to your users in the Microsoft 365 admin center (Billing → Your products). Pro features switch on by themselves for licensed users; there is no key to enter. Each user who opens a report with Pro features needs a licence.

I see a “Pro preview” watermark

You are editing and using a Pro feature without a licence. The watermark names the Pro features the report is using and stays centred without covering the data, so you can try them on your own data. In reading view the free chart is shown without the Pro feature and without the watermark. Remove the field or setting, or buy Pro.

Does it work in Embedded or Publish to Web?

The free features work everywhere. Where Power BI cannot check licences (Publish to Web, embedding, export) the free chart is drawn, without the Pro features and without a watermark.

Does the visual send my data anywhere?

No. It makes no network calls, has no telemetry, sets no cookies and stores nothing outside the report. Your data never leaves Power BI. The links on the start screen open only when you click them, through Power BI. See the privacy policy.
